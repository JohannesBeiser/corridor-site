Questions we have on trail

  • How far to the next water, and how reliable it is right now — hikers' own reports, not just a mapper's guess.
  • What's worth knowing before the next section — permits, river crossings, seasonal closures, long water carries.
  • How far you've walked today and what's left to where you're stopping.
  • What taking an alternate means for you, before committing to it.

Batteries included

  • Missing a trail? — I've built the entire trail-creation process fully automated. All I need as input is the trail's name and a GPX file — that's it. + countless hours reviewing every mile of it
  • Easy water reports — mark a source full, low or dry in two taps, for the next hiker behind you.
  • A push notification the moment you take a wrong turn — the app watches your track and tells you before you've walked far off it.
  • A hand-crafted topo map — Custom made map that adjusts its elevation line labels unit from km/miles, hill-shade adjusts to the rotation of the map
  • Custom alternates — You can draw any line anywhere, following any random trail or completely off trail, and use it as if it were baked into the app, with its entire elevation profile measured. If you like shortcuts the way Pez likes them, you already know
  • A battery-saving mode — no live GPS, and the pixels you don't need switched off.
  • Very high map quality — almost every stream, pass, peak, pond, lake and road is named, so it's easy to write a comment about a place nearby.
  • Offline maps even beyond the trail — at almost no extra data cost (under 5 MB), every map ships with a coarse, 200-mile-wide corridor of roads and general terrain, so you're not stranded the moment you navigate to towns that are further off trail.
  • Waypoint comments — share your insights about a place with other hikers. These also help improving the map in its yearly review
