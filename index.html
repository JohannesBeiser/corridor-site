Questions we have on trail

  • How far to the next water, and how reliable it is right now — hikers' own reports, not just a mapper's guess.

  • What taking an alternate means for you, before committing to it.

Batteries included

Everything a thru-hiking navigation app has to do on a long trail, and the things we kept wishing ours would.

  • Missing a trail? — the entire trail-creation process is built fully automated. All it takes as input is the trail's name and a GPX file — that's it. + countless hours reviewing every mile of it
  • Easy water reports — mark a source full, low or dry in two taps, for the next hiker behind you.
  • Offline maps even beyond the trail — at almost no extra data cost (under 5 MB), every map ships with a coarse, 200-mile-wide corridor of roads and general terrain, so you're not stranded the moment you navigate to towns that are further off trail.
  • Hurt? See how to get off trail asap — no matter where you are, one button shows you the shortest walk out to a road.
  • A push notification the moment you take a wrong turn — the app watches your track and tells you before you've walked far off it.
  • A hand-crafted topo map — Custom made map that adjusts its elevation line labels unit from km/miles, hill-shade adjusts to the rotation of the map
  • Custom alternates — You can draw any line anywhere, following any random trail or completely off trail, and use it as if it were baked into the app, with its entire elevation profile measured. If you like shortcuts the way Pez likes them, you already know
  • A battery-saving mode — no live GPS, and the pixels you don't need switched off.
  • Sunrise and sunset — even offline, see exactly what time the sun rises and sets where you're standing, so you can plan your day around the light.
  • Very high map quality — almost every stream, pass, peak, pond, lake and road is named, so it's easy to write a comment about a place nearby.
  • Waypoint comments — share your insights about a place with other hikers. These also help improving the map in its yearly review

Questions people ask before downloading

What is Corridor?
Corridor is a free offline navigation app for thru-hikers, built by a thru-hiker. The topographic map and the elevation profile sit on one screen and stay in sync, and the whole trail is already on the phone before you start walking. iPhone, iOS 18 and later.
Which trails does it cover?
Long-distance trails, end to end, with every official alternate on each — Te Araroa (3,000 km / 1,864 mi), the Pacific Crest Trail (4,265 km / 2,650 mi) and the Great Divide Trail (1,130 km / 700 mi) among them. Adding a trail takes only a name and a GPX file, so the list keeps growing, and you can draw any line anywhere yourself and use it as if it shipped with the app.
Does it work with no signal?
Yes. Every map is on the phone, not streamed. Each trail ships with a 90 km corridor of topographic detail plus a coarse 200-mile-wide road map around it, so you can still see where you are and how you would walk out, in airplane mode, in a canyon, on day nine.
What does it cost?
Nothing. No account, no subscription, no trial. The app launches on 25 October 2026 and the TestFlight beta is open now.
What does it do that a general hiking app does not?
It only does long trails, so it can answer the questions a thru-hiker actually has: how far to the next water and whether another hiker found it dry, what the next section asks of you, what an alternate costs you in climbing, and the shortest walk out to a road if you get hurt.
Where do the maps come from?
Trail and terrain data from OpenStreetMap contributors and elevation from NASA SRTM and the EU Copernicus programme, rendered into a topographic style built for this app and then reviewed by hand, mile by mile, once a year. Full credits are on the licensing page.
